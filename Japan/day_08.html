WB Studio Tour & Shopping

The Making of Harry Potter, then Tokyo shopping
🇯🇵 Japan· Tokyo Tokyo Tue 4 Feb 2025
If your family loves Harry Potter (and you've already done the Universal rides in Osaka), the Warner Bros. Studio Tour Tokyo is the perfect complement. This isn't a theme park — it's a walk-through experience of the actual sets, props, costumes, and technology used to make the Harry Potter films. Located in Toshimaen, Nerima (northwest Tokyo), it's the same concept as the famous London studio tour but with Japan-exclusive exhibits. Allow 3–4 hours to see everything properly.
🎬 Warner Bros. Studio Tour Tokyo
The tour begins with the iconic doors to the Great Hall swinging open — a genuine goosebumps moment. From there, you walk through Dumbledore's office, the Potions classroom, Diagon Alley, the Forbidden Forest, and dozens of other meticulously recreated sets. The centrepiece is the enormous Hogwarts Castle scale model, lit to simulate day and night. Kids can try riding broomsticks in front of green screens, pull a pint of butterbeer, and see how the special effects were done.
📍 Toshimaen, Nerima, Tokyo ⏱ 3–4 hours 🎟 Timed entry — book online
⚑ Highlights
  • The Great Hall — full-scale film set, doors swing open dramatically
  • Hogwarts Castle scale model — the centrepiece, lit beautifully
  • Diagon Alley — walk down the actual film set
  • Interactive broomstick flying green-screen experience
  • Butterbeer in the backlot
  • The perfect companion to Universal's Harry Potter rides
💡 Guide Tips — WB Studio Tour Tokyo
  • Book tickets online well in advance: This is a timed-entry attraction and popular slots sell out weeks ahead. You cannot buy tickets at the door
  • Getting there: Take the Seibu Toshima Line to Toshimaen Station, or bus from Ikebukuro. The journey from central Tokyo takes about 40–50 minutes
  • Allow 3–4 hours: There's a lot to see. The tour is self-paced so you can linger where the kids are most interested
  • Butterbeer: Available in the mid-tour backlot area. The Japanese version comes in unique flavours
  • Gift shop: The shop at the end is enormous and dangerous for the wallet. Set a souvenir budget beforehand!
  • For younger kids: The Forbidden Forest section can be slightly scary for very young children (moving creatures in the dark). Our 3-year-old was fine but some toddlers might not be
  • Photography: Photos and videos allowed everywhere except a few marked areas. Bring a fully charged phone — you'll take hundreds of photos
  • Vs Universal Osaka: Universal is rides and immersion; the Studio Tour is behind-the-scenes craft and film-making. They complement each other perfectly — do both if you can
🛍 Shopping in Tokyo
After the Studio Tour, we spent the rest of the afternoon shopping in Tokyo. The city is a shopper's paradise — from the massive electronics stores and anime shops of Akihabara to the fashion boutiques of Shibuya and the traditional crafts of Asakusa. Don Quijote (Donki) is a must for families — chaotic multi-floor discount stores packed with snacks, cosmetics, toys, and souvenirs. Japanese Kit-Kats in dozens of unique flavours make perfect gifts. Tax-free shopping is available for tourists spending over ¥5,000 at most stores — bring your passport.
💡 Guide Tips — Shopping in Tokyo with Kids
  • Tax-free: Spend over ¥5,000 at participating stores and show your passport for tax-free prices (8–10% saving). Look for "Tax Free" signs
  • Don Quijote (Donki): Chaotic multi-floor discount stores everywhere in Tokyo. Great for snack packs, cosmetics, toys, and bizarre souvenirs
  • 100-yen shops: Daiso and Seria are Japan's equivalent of dollar stores but the quality is genuinely good. Perfect for small gifts and stationery
  • Souvenir tip: Japanese Kit-Kats come in dozens of unique flavours (matcha, strawberry cheesecake, sake) and make perfect gifts
  • Convenience stores: Don't overlook 7-Eleven, Lawson, and FamilyMart — onigiri, Japanese sandwiches, and treats the kids will love
