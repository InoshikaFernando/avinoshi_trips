Sukiyaki & Sayonara

A farewell feast and the flight to Fiji
🇯🇵 Japan→🇫🇯 Fiji Departure Wed 5 Feb 2025
Our last day in Japan. After an incredible week of theme parks, temples, bullet trains, and city exploring, it was time to say sayonara. We had one final treat — a sukiyaki farewell dinner before heading to the airport for our Fiji Airways flight home via Nadi. Bittersweet, but grateful for every moment.
🥩 Food Highlight — Sukiyaki
Sukiyaki
Sukiyaki is a celebratory hot pot dish — thinly sliced premium beef simmered in a sweet soy-based broth (warishita) with tofu, noodles, mushrooms, and leafy greens. The tradition is to dip each piece in a bowl of beaten raw egg before eating — it sounds unusual but the egg creates a silky, rich coating that is absolutely delicious. It's communal, interactive (everyone cooks at the table), and deeply satisfying. The perfect farewell dinner for a winter trip to Japan.
💡 Guide Tips — Sukiyaki
  • Kid-friendly? Absolutely. The broth is sweet (not spicy), the meat is tender, and kids enjoy the interactive cooking. Skip the raw egg dip for young children if you prefer
  • Where to find it: Look for restaurants with "すき焼き" (sukiyaki) on the sign, or ask your hotel concierge for a recommendation. Many places have picture menus
  • Budget: A good sukiyaki dinner ranges from ¥3,000–¥8,000 per person depending on the quality of beef. Premium wagyu versions are a splurge but unforgettable
  • Vs shabu-shabu: Both are hot pot dishes, but sukiyaki is sweeter and richer (soy-based broth, egg dip), while shabu-shabu is lighter (kombu broth, ponzu/sesame dip). Try both on your trip!
✈ Sayonara, Japan
Route: Tokyo → Nadi (NAN), Fiji
Airline: Fiji Airways
Tip: Check baggage weight limits — Japanese souvenirs and snacks add up fast!
💡 Guide Tips — Departure Day
  • Airport shopping: Narita and Haneda both have excellent duty-free and souvenir shops after security. Japanese sweets, matcha, Kit-Kats, and character goods make great last-minute gifts
  • Food at the airport: Don't waste your last meal on airport fast food — Japanese airports have genuinely good ramen, sushi, and bento options airside
  • Tax refund: If you bought tax-free items during the trip, customs may check them at the airport. Keep your tax-free receipts accessible
  • Kids at the airport: Both Narita and Haneda have play areas and family facilities. Build in buffer time so the kids can burn off energy before the flight
  • Suica/Pasmo cards: If you bought rechargeable train cards, you can use remaining balance at convenience stores in the airport, or keep them for your next trip (they don't expire)
