Editor's Letter

Why Japan, Why Winter

When you're a family of five who genuinely loves to travel, you learn to do it smart. This is the complete journal of our eleven-day winter adventure through Japan — two adults and three kids aged ten, seven, and three — built around theme parks, bullet trains, ancient temples, immersive digital art, and some of the best food we've ever eaten. We did the entire trip with just five hand-luggage bags: no checked baggage, three day kits and two night kits, washed at Japan's brilliant coin laundries every few days. We flew Fiji Airways from New Zealand — not always the cheapest, but a well-timed offer made it so — with a 10+ hour Fiji layover that let us explore Nadi and tick off two countries in one trip. Winter Japan is cold, clear, and gloriously uncrowded. Inside this issue: everything we did, what it cost, what worked, what we'd change, and honest tips for families planning the same journey.
"Two days at Universal, a rickshaw through Kyoto, and a bullet train gliding past a snow-capped Fuji — and still home with nothing but carry-on. Japan rewards families who plan."
The Destinations

Three Cities, One Journey

Each city gave us a completely different side of Japan — neon and novelty, food and warmth, history and calm.
🏙 The Capital
東京 · Tōkyō

Tokyo

The world's largest metropolis is a city of beautiful contradictions — centuries-old shrines tucked between neon skyscrapers, hushed gardens beside roaring intersections. For families, Tokyo is endlessly stimulating: this is where we lost ourselves in the digital universe of teamLab Planets, spent a magical day at Tokyo DisneySea (the only one in the world), and walked the actual film sets at the Warner Bros. Studio Tour.

It's also the easiest of the three cities for English — signage, station staff, and restaurants can generally manage — though a translation app still earns its keep. Tokyo is where our trip began and ended, bookended by bullet-train journeys and a farewell sukiyaki dinner.

🚇 World's busiest rail network 🎢 DisneySea & teamLab 🛍 Shopping paradise
🍜 Kitchen of Japan
大阪 · Ōsaka

Osaka

Loud, warm-hearted and famously food-obsessed, Osaka is Japan's kitchen and its comedy capital. The locals are some of the kindest people we met anywhere — genuinely going out of their way to help — though here the language barrier is real: very little English is spoken, and Google Translate's camera mode became our lifeline for menus and signs.

Osaka was our base for the headline event: two full days at Universal Studios Japan, including the jaw-dropping Wizarding World of Harry Potter. Evenings meant wandering for street food and the freshest sushi of the trip. It's the city our kids still talk about most.

🎢 Universal Studios Japan 🍣 Best sushi of the trip 💬 Big language barrier
⛩ The Old Capital
京都 · Kyōto

Kyoto

Japan's imperial capital for over a thousand years, Kyoto is the country's cultural soul — more than 2,000 temples and shrines, wooden machiya townhouses, geisha districts, and bamboo groves. In winter it's quiet, misty and atmospheric, with far fewer crowds at the famous sites.

Exploring on foot with a three-year-old is a recipe for meltdowns, so our masterstroke was hiring a rickshaw — comfortable for the kids, and our puller doubled as historian and photographer. We attended Sunday Mass here too, in Japan's small but deeply rooted Catholic community. English is better understood in Kyoto than Osaka, a welcome relief.

⛩ 2,000+ temples & shrines 🛺 Rickshaw tours 🍲 Shabu-shabu country

The Food Pages

Eaten & Loved

Four dishes that defined the trip — one in every city we stayed.
1
Sushi
Osaka
The freshest of the trip — melt-in-the-mouth nigiri at a neighbourhood counter, eaten the way it should be: piece by piece, straight from the chef.
2
Shabu-shabu
Kyoto
Paper-thin beef swished through simmering kombu broth, then dipped in ponzu — interactive, warming, and perfect for a cold Kyoto night. The kids loved cooking their own.
3
Ramen
Tokyo
Our welcome-back-to-Tokyo dinner — rich broth, springy noodles, tender chashu. We bumped into one of Avi's friends here, a small-world moment that made the night.
4
Sukiyaki
Tokyo
The farewell feast — premium beef simmered in sweet soy broth and dipped in beaten egg. Celebratory, communal, and the perfect last supper in Japan.
