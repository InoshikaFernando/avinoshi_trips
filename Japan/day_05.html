Kyoto: Rickshaw & Shabu-shabu

Ancient streets by rickshaw
🇯🇵 Japan· Kyoto Kyoto Sat 1 Feb 2025
Kyoto is Japan's cultural heart — a city of over 2,000 temples and shrines, traditional wooden machiya houses, geisha districts, and bamboo groves. But exploring it on foot with three kids (including a 3-year-old) is a recipe for meltdowns. Our solution: hire a rickshaw. It turned out to be one of the smartest decisions of the entire trip. The kids sat comfortably while a skilled rickshaw puller guided us through the narrow streets, stopping at scenic spots and explaining the history. In the evening, we warmed up with shabu-shabu — a perfect winter meal.
🛺 Rickshaw Tour — Arashiyama / Higashiyama
Rickshaw tours in Kyoto are run by fit, knowledgeable young guides who double as photographers, historians, and entertainers. They'll take you through the narrow lanes that buses and taxis can't reach, stop at the best photo spots, and tailor the route to your family's interests. A typical 1–2 hour tour covers the main sights of a district — bamboo groves, temple approaches, traditional streets — at a pace that keeps kids engaged without exhausting anyone. Our toddler loved the ride itself.
📍 Arashiyama or Higashiyama ⏱ 1–2 hours 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Seats 2–3 per rickshaw
🏯 Kyoto's Winter Charm
Kyoto in January/February is cold but beautiful — fewer tourists at temples and cultural sites, misty mornings over bamboo groves, and occasional frost on stone lanterns. The winter light gives everything a serene, ethereal quality. Layer up warmly and bring blankets for the kids in the rickshaw.
💡 Guide Tips — Kyoto Rickshaw with Kids
  • Book ahead: Rickshaw companies like Ebisuya operate in Arashiyama and Higashiyama. Book online for a specific time — walk-ups are possible but you might wait
  • Seating: Each rickshaw seats 2 adults or 1 adult + 2 small children. A family of 5 needs 2 rickshaws, or one adult walks alongside
  • Cost: Expect around ¥5,000–¥10,000 per rickshaw for a 30–60 minute tour. Worth every yen with young kids
  • Photos: The pullers are great photographers — they know the best angles and will use your phone to snap family shots at every stop
  • Why not walk? Kyoto's main temples are spread out. Walking between Kinkaku-ji, Fushimi Inari, and Arashiyama involves significant distances and public transport connections. With a toddler, it's exhausting. A rickshaw covers the highlights of one area comfortably
🍲 Food Highlight — Shabu-shabu
Shabu-shabu
Shabu-shabu is a communal hot pot where you swish paper-thin slices of beef or pork through a pot of simmering kombu broth at the table, then dip them in ponzu or sesame sauce. It's interactive, fun for kids (they can cook their own!), and warming on a cold Kyoto evening. Most restaurants offer all-you-can-eat deals. The meat practically melts in your mouth. We were too busy enjoying the meal to take photos — trust us, just order it!
⚑ Highlights
  • Rickshaw tour through Kyoto's historic streets — kids loved it
  • Traditional temples and scenery without the walking exhaustion
  • Shabu-shabu hot pot dinner — interactive and kid-friendly
  • Winter Kyoto atmosphere — misty, quiet, atmospheric
